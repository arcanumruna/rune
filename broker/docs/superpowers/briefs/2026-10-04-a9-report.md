## Design (as implemented)
- **`detail/RingDoorbell.h`**, part of the ring implementation (atomics allowlisted as RING):
  - One eventfd per consumer thread (a non-blocking pipe off Linux), shared by every ring that thread consumes.
  - Consumer, before a park: store the parking word, `fence(seq_cst)`, then check every watched ring for a
    COMMITTED record at its head (length > 0). If one is there, it does not park. Otherwise it ppolls with the
    doorbell fd in its set. After the park it clears the word and drains the fd only if the fd fired.
  - Producer, after a commit: `fence(seq_cst)`. If the parking word is set, an `exchange` takes it and the fd is
    written. The exchange means several producers write the fd once per park. A producer that finds the consumer
    running pays one fence and one load, no syscall.
  - A claimed-but-uncommitted record is not treated as work. If it were, a late commit would keep the consumer
    re-running passes, which is a spin.
  - A watch can be gated off by its consumer through a `const bool*`. The worker gates its op ring off while the op
    drain is held back (return FIFO at high water, or no session-ack room). Without the gate, a held-back ring would
    keep the runner from ever parking. Each gate has its own wake source.
- **Space wait** (`O2ORingBuffer::armSpaceWait`):
  - The producer sets a want-space word, fences, and re-checks. For queued returns the re-check is the real claim
    (`flushRetFifo_`); for the intake gate it re-reads the ring fill.
  - The consumer, after advancing its head, fences and rings the producer doorbell if the word was set.
  - The worker arms it in `prepareIdle_`, which only runs on passes with no work, when ret_ holds it back.
- **Wiring:**
  - The Worker owns its doorbell. Attached rings: the op ring (with the gate), the session ring, ret_ (as producer,
    for the space wait), and the portable backend's completion ring (so the Docker DR matrix gets a wake too).
  - Fused placement: `BrokerNode::attachFusedRuntimeDoorbell_` attaches the runtime's cross-thread rings to worker 0
    (every other worker's ret_ and the 3 admin → control rings) before those producers start.
  - `ParkUntilWorkIdle` adds the doorbell fd and arms it before each ppoll. `makeShardIdle` takes the doorbell.
  - Proof of path is logged: `idle=wake+doorbell{...}` in the effective config, and
    `park-until-work{wakeFds=4 doorbell=5rings ...}` per worker.
  - `RUNE_IDLE_DOORBELL=0` restores A7 exactly. `RUNE_IDLE_DOORBELL_MIN_PARK_US` / `RUNE_IDLE_DOORBELL_PARK_US`
    default to 1000 / 1000. Garbage refuses startup.
- **Park timeout from deadlines: not done, on purpose.**
  - No deadline on the worker path is below the 1 ms bound, so a next-deadline query would never bind. The Rune
    ones are: fetch backstop 5 ms, election status 20 ms, log sync 10 ms, ack timeout 30 s.
  - The timers that remain are inside Aeron's C driver and client (NAK 60 ms, SM 200 ms, heartbeats 100 ms and up,
    timer interval 1 s, keepalive 500 ms), which expose no deadline without patching Aeron.
  - The fixed 1 ms bound costs about 860 wakes/s per idle worker, which is the 10% idle floor measured below.
- **Found and fixed while auditing what a long park could stall:**
  - Worker-path Aeron claims surfaced ADMIN_ACTION (a term rotation) as a refusal. The claim was retried only on the
    next pass, which after a park can be up to the park cap.
  - Affected sites: SessionAckSink acks, WorkerReplicationTransport push/fetch, WorkerEgress, RecordsPush and
    Worker::sendEncodedToPub_.
  - Each now retries once in place, which is Aeron's contract for ADMIN_ACTION.
  - `ClientGateway` and `AeronPartitionTransport` already fell through to `offer()`, so they were left alone.

Why B pays p50:
- At 20k an event arrives every ~70 µs.
- A's 30-pass spin/yield phase (~300 µs of polling) spans that gap, so A never parks (36-134 vcsw/s per worker).
- B parks after every empty pass (464-1,313 vcsw/s), so each event pays a park wake.
- The box is saturated (system idle 2-6%). When a worker's fd fires, its core is often running the unpinned client
  or softirq.

The probe (single B runs) showed that doubling the park from 1 µs instead of starting at 1 ms changes nothing (p50
467 vs 453 µs).

Multi-worker arm (2 workers per node): **not run**.
- The harness refuses the layout on this box: "3 nodes x 2 workers from core 1 needs cores 1..6 but the box has 4".
- Coverage of the cross-thread path instead: `rune_shard_mode_tests` on Linux (3 nodes x 2 fused workers, real UDP)
  and the Linux parked-shard-runner test.

VM benchmark time: ~28 min (3 probes, the 10-run A/B, 2 refused multi-worker runs, 2 B-hyb runs). Everything ran
under flock with vm-clean. Linux test builds and runs were also under flock. /root/a9 is deleted; 13 GB free.

## Concerns
- **The goal is not met.** The 20k CPU that A spends above B is the polling that buys its p50 on a saturated
  4-core box. The doorbell removes the correctness reason for the spin/yield phase, not the latency reason.
  - Measured trade for the 0/0 shape: -6.3 points of worker CPU, -5.6 points of client CPU, +109 µs p50.
  - The shipped default keeps the phase. 0/0 stays an opt-in with `RUNE_IDLE_SPINS=0 RUNE_IDLE_YIELDS=0`.
  - What would move 20k CPU: fewer events per record, a cheaper pass, or headroom (the client off the worker cores).
- **The shipped default was measured once per scenario**, through env on the A/B binary: B-hyb idle and 20k, one
  run each, not interleaved. Its 20k p99 (9.5 ms) and p50 (356 µs) are single runs.
- **`ShardModeRegression.SlowEndpointUnawareClientCannotStarveTheShard` is flaky on Linux in both arms.** It fails
  3 of 20 isolated runs on A and 3 of 20 on B. This is the pre-existing KNOWN ISSUE written in the test: the pause
  depends on pipeline depth.
  - A's failures show `ackRoomOpPauses` around 31k, and in one run `awaitProduceResponse` returned false, i.e. an
    ack did not arrive. That could be a real lost ack; it is pre-existing and belongs to the T13 owner.
  - B's failures are different: the gateway pause did not engage within 40k frames. Plausible cause: the
    ADMIN_ACTION retry removes spurious "Held" signals on term rotation, so the pause now waits for real
    back-pressure. Not proven.
  - Separately, `AdvertisedIngressRoutesAreLiveForEndpointUnawareAndAwareClients` failed once (after 32 s) in the
    first full B run. It then passed 20 of 20 isolated runs and 4 more full runs.
- **The fused worker 0 watches 5 rings.** The cleaner's view-ack ring and the signal ring are not attached: both are
  cold, and both are bounded by the 1 ms park.
- **The thread-placement runtime thread does not park on a doorbell.** It keeps its backoff. Only shard runners use
  the doorbell.
- Planka MCP failed to connect this session, so there are no ticket moves.

### What changed
- **M-1. `RUNE_IDLE_DOORBELL=0` is A7 again.**
  - The Worker no longer wires its doorbell in its constructor.
  - `WorkerPool::enableShardDoorbells` wires it only when the runner will park on it
    (`shardRunnerUsesDoorbell`: wake fds + doorbell on + not busy-spin). It is called from `BrokerNode::start`
    before the control thread, the runners and the admin thread exist, and before any io submission.
  - With the doorbell off, nothing is wired: commits and reads pay no doorbell fence, `prepareIdle_` arms
    nothing, and no fd is ever written.
  - The fused-runtime attach follows worker 0's state.
  - The pipe doorbell (non-Linux) now writes 1 byte. EAGAIN means the pipe is already readable, and the
    consumer drains until EAGAIN when it wakes. Nothing writes to an unpolled fd any more.
- **M-2. Space wait on the op and session rings.**
  - A refused claim on a ring that has a producer doorbell now arms the space wait inside `tryClaim`, then
    re-claims once (the Dekker re-check). Every producer retry loop therefore wakes on freed space, with no
    change at the call sites.
  - Fused placement: worker 0's doorbell is the producer doorbell of every other worker's op and session rings,
    so the runtime's queued role, Subscribe, produce and announce retries no longer wait out a 1 ms park.
- **L-1.** Fused worker 0's own ret_ gets no space doorbell, because its producer and consumer are the same
  thread.
- **L-2.** The space-wait word now sits on its own cache line.
- **L-5.** Added a test comment: the ping-pong stress guards the fences only on x86-64 Linux (the VM suite).
