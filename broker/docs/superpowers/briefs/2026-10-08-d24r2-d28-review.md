## MAJOR

### P1-MAJOR-1 (new): TermFloorNotice reopens partial-partition leadership ping-pong. A candidate cut off from the leader, plus a bridge node in CANVASS, can depose the majority's leader repeatedly, so convergence depends on timing (Election.cpp:979-986, 1254-1274, 778-785)

**How the notice works.** The notice turns any floor raise at a non-sticky
node into a step-down of a leader that has an active quorum. The round-1
argument was "each round needs a fresh real grant". But the step-down itself
keeps re-creating the conditions for that fresh grant:

- the bridge node V stays in CANVASS while the majority re-elects;
- a candidate X that is cut off from the leader keeps standing at increasing
  terms. Its own candidacies never raise its floor, and it never hears the
  leader.

**5-node failure scenario.** Nodes L, A, B, V, X. Only the X–L link is down.
L leads term T with the active quorum {L, A, B}.

1. V restarts, or its liveness flaps once, and enters CANVASS. X is
   canvassing because it cannot hear L.
2. X stands at f. V is not sticky in CANVASS and grants it, so V's floor is
   f. A and B are sticky CLOSED followers and deny. X's ballot fails.
3. V returns to CANVASS. L's NLT for T is below V's floor, so V sends a
   notice. L has an active quorum, but steps down to f (onTermFloorNotice →
   `leaveClosedForElection_(f)`).
4. A and B keep denying everyone, including L, until their heartbeat timeout
   expires (see P1-MEDIUM-2). L is the only node at candidate f, so it stands
   at f+1 about one to 1.5 election timeouts later. If A and B are still
   sticky at that point, L stands at f+2 later still.
5. Meanwhile X stands at f+1, then f+2, about every two election timeouts.
   V is in CANVASS or FOLLOWER_BALLOT for the whole re-election and grants
   whichever request arrives first.
6. Whenever X reaches a term above the majority's newly elected term N before
   N's NLT reaches V, V's floor exceeds N. V refuses N, sends a notice, and
   the new leader steps down. Back to step 3.

**Consequences:**

- Every round is a full write outage of the consensus leader: heartbeat
  timeout, plus canvass, plus nomination, plus ballot.
- Termination depends on which of two random walks wins a race. It
  converges with probability 1, but there is no bound. That violates the
  time-independent convergence rule.
- Before the notice, the same topology cost one stranded node (V) and no
  outages.
- This is the etcd "CheckQuorum without PreVote" disruptive-node class. etcd
  documents that both features are needed.

**Other cases checked and found safe:**

- **Asymmetric links:** L→V broken means no NLT, so no notice. V→L broken
  means the notice is lost, so V stays stranded as before. Neither ping-pongs.
- **A floor adopted from a deposed leader's stale message:** a floor comes
  from a grant, or from a leader that won a majority ballot. A leader below a
  majority-elected floor is a zombie, so stepping it down is correct.
- **Duplicate or old notices:** `refusedTermId != leadershipTermId_` drops
  them.
- **A notice that reaches a leader in LeaderLogReplication, LeaderReplay,
  LeaderInit or LeaderReady:** these take the same path as A1, which is
  fine.
- **Resigned (StandDown) leaders:** `leaderMember_ == nullptr`, so the notice
  is ignored.
- **Is it safe to step down to `floorTermId`?** Yes.
  - The floor is raised durably before the term is adopted. The next
    election is at a term above f, so the node never stands at a term it
    adopted.
  - Ack term = f, and the next epoch is at least f+1, so nothing freezes.
  - If a real leader of term f exists, the stepped-down node follows it
    (equal term, not below its floor).

**Fix (pick one; the first is the principled fix):**

1. **PreVote (Raft §9.6).** A candidate first runs a non-binding pre-ballot at
   `candidate+1`. Nobody raises a term or a floor during it. A voter refuses a
   pre-vote if it heard a live leader within `leaderHeartbeatTimeoutNs_`.
   That includes a leader whose NLT it *refused*, and it applies in every
   state, not only CLOSED.
   - X can then never collect a pre-vote majority, so it never bumps a real
     term, and V's floor never ratchets.
   - This also removes the pre-existing "cut-off node climbs terms" noise.
2. **Interim narrowing, if PreVote is deferred. Do all three:**
   - **(a)** On a notice step-down from CLOSED, broadcast `StandDown(T)` to the
     followers. This uses the existing template 59, so they canvass at once
     instead of after the heartbeat timeout.
   - **(b)** In CLOSED stickiness (line 778), grant a RequestVote whose
     candidate **is the leader we follow**: it has evidently stepped down.
   - **(c)** A CANVASS, NOMINATE or FOLLOWER_BALLOT node that heard a
     leader's NLT within `leaderHeartbeatTimeoutNs_` denies (with no
     term/floor raise) RequestVotes from any member *other than that leader*.

   With (a) to (c), the majority usually re-elects within one nomination delay,
   while V still denies X. That shrinks the window, but it does not close it.
   Record the residual in the backlog.

**Regression test (deterministic, ElectionLog multi-instance harness):**

- 5 Elections wired through fake publishers with the X↔L edge dropped. V is
  restarted into CANVASS.
- X keeps canvassing.
- Assert that `termFloorStepDownsForTest()` summed over all nodes is at most
  1, and that some leader then holds CLOSED for at least 20 heartbeat
  intervals.
- Run it with jittered RNG seeds (e.g. 200 seeds) so the race is actually
  explored.

## MEDIUM

### P1-MEDIUM-1: The legacy (pre-D24) floor slot is never migrated unless the node raises the floor above its candidate term. Upgraded nodes keep the pre-D24 rule across restarts, and the notice turns that into needless leader step-downs (NodeStateFile.cpp:161-185)

The read side works: raw 0 means the floor is the candidate term. But
`proposeMaxFollowFloorTermId` writes the slot only when
`term > followFloorTermId()`. On a legacy file that reads the *live*
`candidateTermId()`, so a follow at T below the candidate leaves raw 0 on
disk.

**Sequence:**

1. Upgrade with candidate C0.
2. Own failed candidacies raise the candidate to C0+2. The legacy floor
   "rises" with it, because it is read as the candidate.
3. The node follows a leader at T, with C0 ≤ T < C0+2. In memory the floor is
   T. On disk the slot is still 0.
4. Restart. The floor reads C0+2 > T, so the node refuses the live leader.
5. That is the exact D24 stranding. With the notice it now **deposes** a
   healthy leader instead of stranding.

This repeats on every restart until a grant or adoption above the candidate
happens. The floor also silently absorbs own failed candidacies, which D24
says must never raise it.

**Fix:** migrate in the `NodeStateFile` constructor:

- On an existing file whose raw slot is 0, store `candidateTermId + kFloorBias`
  and sync once. That freezes the conservative floor at upgrade time; later
  own candidacies no longer move it.
- Test: on a legacy file, `proposeMaxCandidateTermId(9)`, then
  `proposeMaxFollowFloorTermId(7)`, then reopen. Assert floor == the
  migrated value (or 7), not 9.

### P1-MEDIUM-2: A notice step-down costs a full heartbeat timeout of headlessness, because the deposed leader's own followers deny it (Election.cpp:1266-1273 with 762-785)

`onTermFloorNotice` → `leaveClosedForElection_(f)` sends nothing to the
followers. They stay CLOSED and sticky (`followerLiveness_` armed, last
heartbeat recent). So they deny every RequestVote, **including the one from
the leader they follow**, until `leaderHeartbeatTimeoutNs_` expires.

The leader's own f+1 candidacy usually fails once, and the outage becomes
heartbeat timeout + 1-2 ballots.

This is also the window that feeds P1-MAJOR-1.

**Fix:** items (a) and (b) of P1-MAJOR-1's interim fix:

- StandDown broadcast on a notice step-down;
- stickiness exemption for a candidacy from our own leader.

Both are cheap and use existing messages. Test: a 3-node harness. After the
notice, assert a new leader within 2 election timeouts, with the heartbeat
timeout set to 10× the election timeout.

The round-2 ODR incident proved the hazard: a vtable without

**Gaps:**

1. **SBE wire:** nothing checks that `SbeConsensusPublisher::termFloorNotice`

3. **Convergence:** round 1 asked for a 5-node test in which V reaches CLOSED

# PART 2: D28 (MappedFile prefault actually prefaults)

## Verdict

- **The root cause is right,** and the volatile fix to `MappedFile` is
  correct for the case that matters in production: the provisioner's fresh
  staging `.index` (prepareStaging, LogSegment.cpp:95).
- **The second half of the change, as described, does not exist.** Log's
  recovery still constructs every recovered segment with
  `/*prefaultIndex*/false`. Only a comment was added.
- **The new regression test for that half cannot fail.**
- **The read-touch/write-touch split is keyed on file size.** For an index,
  that is the wrong boundary.

## Volatile correctness

`(void)v[off]` and `v[off] = v[off]` through a `volatile uint8_t*` are both
mandatory side effects, so the compiler can neither drop nor merge them:

- `[expr.context]/2`: a discarded-value subscript of a volatile glvalue
  undergoes lvalue-to-rvalue conversion.
- Each volatile access occurs exactly once, in order.
- The simple assignment is not one of the C++20-deprecated volatile forms.

Other parts of the new prefault logic:

- **`existingBytes` from `lseek` before `ftruncate`:** correct.
- **`min(existingBytes, size_)`:** keeps a read-only mapping that is larger
  than EOF from touching past EOF (no SIGBUS). That is good, since read-only
  mappings are now eligible; today no caller passes `prefault=true` with
  `writable=false`.
- **The read-only touch of existing content:** removes the lost-update hazard
  that the old `data_[off] = data_[off]` would have had on a re-mapped file
  someone else writes. Debug builds actually ran that path.
- **The borrowed-fd constructor and `resize()`:** never prefaulted, before or
  after. That is unchanged and correct: the grow paths are rare and are sized
  for the overshoot cap.

## MAJOR

### P2-MAJOR-1: "Recovery constructs recovered segments with prefaultIndex=true" is not in the code, and the regression test for it is vacuous (Log.cpp:170-178; LogSegment.cpp:917-918; LogSegmentTest.cpp:991-1022)

**The change is missing.**

- The hunk at Log.cpp:170-174 adds only a comment. Line 177 still passes
  `/*prefaultIndex*/false`. So do 717, 1864, 2009 and 2082.
- The comment states behaviour that does not exist.

**Even if line 177 were flipped, it would be wasted work.**

- `Log::recover()` → `validateFromRecoveryPoint_` calls
  `LogSegment::recover()` on *every* segment, in both the trusted and the
  validated region.
- That function rebuilds `.index.tmp`, renames it, and **replaces** `index_`
  with a fresh `OffsetIndex(indexPath, …)` that has the default
  `prefault=false` (line 917).
- So the constructor's prefaulted mapping is thrown away.

**The test cannot fail.**

- Line 918's `rebuildEntries()` scans the index from slot 0 to the first
  zero entry. That read-maps **every populated page** in the process's mm,
  on the recovery thread.
- So `readInto(1, …)` binary-searches already-mapped pages, and
  `ReadIntoOnARecoveredSegmentNeverFaultsTheIndexRegression` is green with or
  without any prefault.
- Its premise ("recovery's last-entry read touches only the last page") is
  false for the current recovery path.
- It never went RED, which violates the repro-first rule.

**What actually still faults on the owner after a restart** is the
**recovered ACTIVE segment's append tail**:

- pages past `entries_ * 8` in the final mapping (line 917) are untouched;
- each `OffsetIndex::append` into a new page is a fault on the owner;
- that is the D28 symptom, for the first segment after every restart.

**Fix:**

- Drop the Log.cpp comment.
- Prefault where the index is final: after `rebuildEntries()` in
  `LogSegment::recover()` (and `openTrusted`), for the active segment only.
  Write-touch from the page holding `entries_ * kEntrySize` to `size()`, via
  a new `MappedFile::prefaultWrite(from, to)` / `OffsetIndex::prefaultTail()`.
  Sealed indexes need nothing: `rebuildEntries` already mapped their
  populated pages.
- Replace the test with one that is RED today. After recover, measure
  faults across N appends that cross index pages on the active segment. Use
  pagemap or `RUSAGE_THREAD` (see P2-LOW-3).

## MEDIUM

### P2-MEDIUM-1: Keying the read/write split on file size means a writable mapping whose existing bytes will be appended to gets only read-touched. Every first write is then a write-protect fault, the same mmap_lock/VMA-lock path D28 removes (MappedFile.cpp:33-37, 54-59)

**Why the split goes wrong for indexes.**

- Index files are created by `ftruncate` to their full `indexMaxBytes`, both
  the staging file and `recover()`'s `.index.tmp`.
- So on any reopen, `existingBytes == size_`, and the *whole* mapping counts
  as "existing". It is read-touched only.

**Why read-touching is not enough.**

- In a `MAP_SHARED` writable mapping of an ext4 file, a read fault installs a
  read-only PTE (write-notify / `page_mkwrite`).
- The owner's first append into each page then takes `do_wp_page` →
  `ext4_page_mkwrite`: a fault, plus a journal handle, plus delalloc
  reservation for a hole.
- That is a regression versus the intended write-touch for any caller that
  writes into "existing" bytes. Today that caller would be the recovered
  active index if P2-MAJOR-1 is fixed by passing `prefault=true` to the
  constructor.
- The fresh staging file is unaffected: `existingBytes = 0`, so everything is
  write-touched, which is correct.

**Fix:**

- MappedFile cannot know the logical end. Let the caller supply it:
  `prefault(writeFrom)`, where OffsetIndex passes `entries_ * kEntrySize`.
- Alternatively, keep the constructor flag for fresh files only and add the
  explicit tail-prefault from P2-MAJOR-1.
- Document in `MappedFile.h` that `prefault=true` on an existing file never
  makes existing pages writable without a fault.

### P2-MEDIUM-2: If recovery prefaults every recovered index as the comment describes, startup cost and page-cache residency scale with total data (Log.cpp:170-178)

**The cost.**

- `indexMaxBytesFor` gives about 2 × (roll / 4096) × 8 bytes per segment,
  with a 16 KiB minimum. That is roughly data/256 of index.
- About half of that is ftruncate holes. Read-faulting a hole in a shared
  file mapping allocates a zeroed page-cache page.
- So prefaulting every recovered index means about data/256 of RAM touched,
  and about data/256/4096 minor faults at startup:
  - 1 TiB per broker → about 4 GiB and about 1M faults;
  - the 40k-segment case from D20 → at least 160k faults.
- All of it is clean, so it is reclaimable, and under memory pressure it is
  evicted anyway.
- Since `recover()` replaces each index (P2-MAJOR-1), it would also be done
  twice.

**Fix:** do not prefault sealed indexes; prefault only the active segment's
tail. If someone later wants sealed-index warmth for catch-up reads, use
`MADV_WILLNEED` or `MADV_POPULATE_READ` on the populated range only.

## LOW

- **P2-LOW-1 (MappedFile.cpp:45-60): prefer
  `madvise(MADV_POPULATE_READ / MADV_POPULATE_WRITE)` (Linux ≥ 5.14).**
  - One syscall instead of N user-mode faults.
  - It takes the mmap lock once.
  - It cannot be elided by any optimizer.
  - It returns an error instead of SIGBUS past EOF.
  - Keep the volatile loop as the `EINVAL` fallback. The project already
    targets io_uring kernels, so 5.14+ is likely.
- **P2-LOW-2 (behaviour now actually shipping): the write-touch now runs in
  Release for the first time.**
  - What it costs: every staging index's zero tail is dirtied. With the 2×
    headroom, about half of each index is zeros, and the seal-time index
    sync now writes back the full `indexMaxBytes` per segment instead of
    only the populated pages. Dirty pages also count toward
    `balance_dirty_pages` on the provisioner.
  - Expected effect: small, about 0.4% of segment bytes, and off the owner.
  - What to do: confirm it with a single seal-sync latency A/B, and note it in
    the perf findings spec, per the document-perf rule.
- **P2-LOW-3 (LogSegmentTest.cpp:1016):** `RUSAGE_SELF` counts faults from
  every thread (any provisioner, cleaner or gtest thread), so it is flaky by

  - **It does not verify "never dirtied".** To check that: write `4` to

    bit 55 (soft-dirty) is clear on the existing-content pages after the

  The control probe is sound: bit 63 is visible unprivileged, and an
  untouched mapping reads 0.
- **P2-LOW-5 (MappedFile.cpp:1):** `#include <algorithm>` was added above
  the file's own header. Keep the own header first, so it stays
  self-contained-checked.
